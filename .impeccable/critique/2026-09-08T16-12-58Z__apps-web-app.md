## Design Specificity

**LLM assessment:** Leonly feels authored for a private, two-person keepsake product. Fraunces, warm paper surfaces, milestones, paired avatars, and the shared/vault distinction form a coherent emotional world. Specificity weakens at the edges: generic Unsplash couples, administrative creation mechanics, and inconsistent trust/error handling could belong to any lifestyle app.

## Overall Impression

The product already has a recognizable soul. The biggest opportunity is to make its behavior as trustworthy as its visual promise: protect unfinished memories, explain privacy precisely, and remove generic third-party cues from the entry experience.

## What's Working

- The dashboard’s milestone, paired avatars, and recent-memory hierarchy create an emotionally appropriate return moment rather than a generic analytics dashboard.
- Mobile bottom navigation, desktop sidebar, safe-area handling, visible current location, and reduced-motion variants establish a strong responsive foundation.
- Memory detail gives photography, chronology, comments, and related memories enough space to feel reflective instead of transactional.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Unfinished memories can be lost during internal navigation

**Why it matters:** Creating a memory is emotionally and mechanically expensive. A refresh warning alone does not protect users who tap Back, Cancel, or app navigation.

**Fix:** Persist fields and photo metadata locally, restore interrupted drafts, and intercept internal exits with “Keep editing” / “Discard draft.” Cover navigation, refresh, and remount recovery in tests.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] OAuth initiation can fail without visible recovery

**Why it matters:** A silent failure on the first product action damages trust before the private space exists.

**Fix:** Render a localized `role="alert"` beside the Google action, keep retry enabled, and distinguish offline/provider failures from generic failures.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] The privacy-first entry screen depends on generic third-party romance imagery

**Why it matters:** Public CDN requests and interchangeable stock couples contradict the “private sanctuary” positioning at its highest-trust moment.

**Fix:** Self-host first-party imagery or replace literal couples with a distinctive Leonly keepsake composition.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable bolder`

### [P2] The empty timeline explains but does not activate

**Why it matters:** A new couple reaches a dead end precisely when the product should help them preserve their first memory. The vault already handles this better.

**Fix:** Add one burgundy “Preserve your first memory” action and use context-specific copy for dashboard recents versus the full timeline.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable onboard`

### [P2] The documented design system is not a dependable source of truth

**Why it matters:** Hundreds of detector advisories are noisy because prose and machine-readable tokens disagree. Real drift becomes hard to distinguish from intentional exceptions.

**Fix:** Reconcile approved colors, sizes, radii, shadows, and lightbox exceptions with the structured design contract; then address the width animation and decorative auth grid.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable document`

## Emotional Journey

- Peak: the dashboard milestone and memory detail make the relationship, not the software, the protagonist.
- Valley: the long creation form feels fragile because interruption can destroy work.
- Valley: anonymous stock couples make the sign-in screen feel borrowed.
- Reassurance: deletion, conflict, unavailable-resource, and vault states generally explain what remains protected.
- Missing reassurance: privacy boundaries and OAuth failures are weakest at the exact moments users need certainty.

## Persona Red Flags

- **Alex, power user:** no accelerated repeat-entry path, search, draft continuation, or batch-friendly photo controls; every photo exposes separate actions.
- **Sam, keyboard/screen-reader user:** the broad semantic foundation is good, but silent OAuth failure is not announced and icon-dependent detail actions require perfect accessible-name coverage.
- **Casey, distracted mobile user:** interruption can erase a long memory draft; ten-photo action density is poor for one-handed use; remote auth imagery adds avoidable network dependence.
- **Jordan, first-timer:** “Private Vault” does not immediately state “hidden from the timeline, still shared with your partner,” and the empty timeline lacks an obvious next action.

## Minor Observations

- Creation allows ten photos while editing allows five without explaining the constraint.
- “Active” beside both members can read as live presence rather than active membership.
- Routine entrance motion is well reduced for accessibility but may still make frequent navigation feel overly ceremonial.
- Terms and Privacy affordances need to be real, localized destinations rather than decorative trust copy.

## Questions To Consider

- If Leonly promises sanctuary, why does its first screen borrow anonymous couples from a public image CDN?
- What changes when “we never lose your story” becomes visible product behavior through drafts and restoration?
- Should the one-member dashboard prioritize partner activation over relationship celebration?
- Is “Private Vault” the clearest name for “off the shared timeline, still visible to both partners”?
